3 Fears About Hiring a Babysitter in India

Parenting 2 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · Team Pacifyco

Every parent who has ever thought about hiring a babysitter has also lain awake thinking about it. That is not paranoia, but love doing its job.

So this piece will not tell you not to be afraid, but will share some perspectives that may nip your fear in the bud. By the end, we suspect you will feel what many parents feel: why didn't we have this before?

Fear 1: "What if something happens to my children?"

Yes, we have all seen and read the news articles on unpleasant incidents with a heavy heart. But this was never exclusive to nannies or babysitters. Unfortunately, daycares, schools, school vans, and sometimes families are all prey to such incidents. So, the danger was never the word "babysitter" but an unchecked person, in any role, with no one watching.

Look at how the rest of the world built safety nets for such incidents rather than closing the door. In the UK, childcare agencies screen carers through the government's DBS criminal record checks; in Australia, they run a Working With Children Check for people who work with kids; and in the US, families routinely ask for background checks, references and first-aid certificates before a sitter enters their home.

And here is something many of you may not realise: we have safety nets too.

  • Police verification. Anyone you employ at home, a cook, a driver, a nanny, can be verified with the local police.
  • Strong child protection law. The POCSO Act, 2012 treats offences against children with some of the strictest penalties in Indian law.
  • A helpline that answers. Child Helpline 1098 is now linked to the 112 emergency system, with control rooms set up in all 36 states and union territories.

This doesn't mean one's fears are silly, but most of these fears can be soothed by stringent background verification, whether you are hiring a babysitter, a full-time nanny, a driver or a tutor.

What does "properly checked" include?

  • Identity and address verification
  • Police verification
  • A check on education and references
  • A psychologist screening, to understand temperament and patience
  • A health self-declaration
  • An in-person meeting with you before the first session

Fear 2: "Young people today are careless and they're always on their phones."

This worry is as old as the oldest worry in the world, because every generation has said this about the next.

Look at the young generation of today. They have raised their voice against injustice, they speak with utmost clarity, they try to maintain composure and dignity in an argument, they aren't unnecessarily judgmental and are emotionally aware, they aren't reckless when human life is involved, and much more. These are your sitters. Each of them grew up in a home very much like yours, and was taught to greet elders, to share her tiffin, to help out at family functions and to call every adult "aunty" or "uncle". You both were raised on the same values.

Now if you'd like to see the data to back it up, here you go:

  • They are deeply family-minded. In the 2026 Family First Global Survey, 84% of Indian Gen Z said spending time with family is very important, the highest share of any country surveyed.
  • They want to learn by doing. Deloitte's 2025 survey found 94% of Indian Gen Zs value practical, on-the-job learning over theory. Looking after a child is exactly that kind of learning.
  • They care about doing the right thing. The same Deloitte research shows a large share of Indian Gen Z have made career choices based on personal ethics.

Having said that, there will always be a few bad apples. But that is what we have a process for: to make sure you don't get to see them.

As for the phone, you both own the device and use it as per your convenience. So, the difference between a careless babysitter and a good one is not the phone but giving them exceptional training and clear expectations. A well-trained sitter knows the phone is for emergencies and updates, not scrolling.

Fear 3: "What will people say?"

This question comes from one of the most powerful committees in India.

A relative yapping about why you need "outside help", a neighbour prodding about the new face at the door, or a mother-in-law with pointed anecdotes about how she managed three children without anyone's help. But the loudest of all would be the voice in your own head asking whether a "good mother" would ever want a few hours to herself.

Here is what we forget most times. The women who raised us had a village around them, most of the time. Their mothers were down the corridor and neighbours were always available to help. That support was invisible because it was free. Today, with nuclear families, that has largely disappeared. So asking for help is simply rebuilding what earlier generations took for granted.

The conversation is changing among many young parents today. Mothers are talking openly in apartment groups and online communities about exhaustion, postpartum blues and the need for a break to focus on their mental and physical well-being. Every mother who says "I hired a sitter, and it changed everything" makes it easier for the next one to shift her thinking.

As for mom guilt: rest is not a luxury, it is a necessity. A rested mother is more patient, more playful and more herself, and that is exactly the mother your child needs.

And the Quieter Worries

Beyond the big three, there are smaller worries.

"I don't like a stranger in my house." That's fair, but a verified babysitter at home is a stranger only on day one. You can have house rules and keep certain spaces off-limits, and be there with the sitter for the first few days to gauge your own comfort with the arrangement.

"Will she respect our food, faith and way of doing things?" All you need to do is tell her and she will. Whether you are vegetarian, pray at sunset or have strict no-screen rules before dinner, a good sitter treats house rules as part of the job.

"My child is shy. She won't go to anyone." Many children are wary at first, but a young person will usually find creative ways to engage children. Start with a short session while you are around, and let the sitter take things slowly. Shy children often become the most attached once the ice breaks.

"My husband or parents aren't convinced." Meet the sitter once, do the first session at home, and let the experience speak for itself. Doubts tend to soften when the child comes running to say they had a wonderful time.

"Isn't this an indulgence?" With a babysitting service, you pay only for the hours you use, with no long-term commitment. It is time to work, recover, see a doctor, sleep or simply be your own person again. This is also important for a healthy relationship with your child.

So, Why Didn't We Have This Before?

Think back on each fear and you will notice that none of them is really a reason to say no. Each one is a reason to choose carefully: verify properly, meet first, share your rules and stay involved until the arrangement feels comfortable.

A cheerful, trained babysitter who arrives on time, plays with your child, keeps her safe and keeps you updated is all you need for a few hours of guilt-free me-time.
